How take-home pay works

Your gross pay is not what lands in your bank account. Before you are paid, several amounts come off it:

  • Federal income tax — 2026 has seven marginal rates (10% to 37%). Only the income inside each bracket is taxed at that bracket's rate. The 2026 standard deduction ($16,100 single / $32,200 married filing jointly) comes off first, so a single filer on $60,000 is taxed on $43,900, not $60,000.
  • Social Security (6.2%) — charged on wages up to the 2026 wage base of $184,500. Above that, no more Social Security tax for the year.
  • Medicare (1.45%) — charged on all wages, with no cap. High earners pay an extra 0.9% on wages over $200,000 (single) or $250,000 (married filing jointly).
  • State income tax — ranges from 0% (Texas, Washington and seven other states levy no tax on wages) to California's 13.3% top rate. Some states also charge payroll levies, such as California SDI (1.3%) and Washington's PFML and WA Cares — most generic calculators leave these out, so their state numbers are wrong.
  • Pre-tax deductions — a traditional 401(k) lowers your federal and state taxable income (but not your Social Security/Medicare wages). Roth 401(k) contributions lower neither.

Because the rates are marginal, a pay rise never makes you poorer: only the dollars above each threshold are taxed at the higher rate.

What this calculator does NOT model

  • State income tax for states other than CA, TX, WA, NY and NM — for those states the result is a federal + FICA estimate only, and the page says so.
  • City and local income taxes, except New York City (toggle above). This includes Philadelphia, most Ohio and Kentucky cities, and Yonkers (16.75% surcharge on NY State tax).
  • Tax credits — child tax credit, earned income tax credit, education credits, and the NYC school tax credit are not included.
  • New York benefit recapture above $107,650 of income, and the NY Paid Family Leave premium — both are real costs for some New Yorkers and are not in the model yet.
  • Itemised deductions, multiple jobs, mid-year pay changes, bonuses (bonuses are withheld federally at a flat 22% supplemental rate), tips, overtime premiums, garnishments, and self-employment tax (SECA 15.3%).
  • California HSA treatment — California does not recognise HSA contributions, so HSA money entered as an "other pre-tax deduction" lowers the federal figure but should still count as California wages. The calculator does not split this out.
  • Withholding vs actual liability — this tool estimates annual tax liability spread across paychecks. Actual withholding follows IRS Publication 15-T tables and your W-4, and your refund or balance due at filing time settles the difference.
